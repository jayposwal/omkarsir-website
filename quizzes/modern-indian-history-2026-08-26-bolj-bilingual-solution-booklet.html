व्याख्या
1453 ई. में ऑटोमन तुर्कों द्वारा बैजंटाइन साम्राज्य की राजधानी कुस्तुंतुनिया पर अधिकार कर लेने के कारण यूरोप का लाल सागर और फारस की खाड़ी वाला पारंपरिक स्थल व्यापारिक मार्ग बंद हो गया था। इसके बाद यूरोप में पुनर्जागरण एवं वैज्ञानिक प्रगति के परिणामस्वरूप नए समुद्री मार्गों की खोज शुरू हुई। पुर्तगाल के राजकुमार 'हेनरी द नेविगेटर' के प्रोत्साहन से 1488 में बार्थोलोम्यू डियास अफ्रीका के दक्षिणी सिरे (केप ऑफ गुड होप/केप ऑफ स्टॉर्म्स) तक पहुँचा। 7 जून 1494 को स्पेन और पुर्तगाल के मध्य 'टॉरडेसिलस की संधि' हुई, जिसके तहत केप वर्डे द्वीप से 370 लीग पश्चिम में काल्पनिक रेखा खींचकर पूर्व का भाग पुर्तगाल और पश्चिम का भाग स्पेन को सौंपा गया। कथन D असत्य है क्योंकि 1492 में स्पेन के ध्वज तले निकले क्रिस्टोफर कोलंबस ने भारत की खोज में अमेरिका (वेस्टइंडीज/बहामास) की खोज की थी, जबकि ब्राजील की खोज 1500 ई. में पुर्तगाली नाविक पेड्रो अल्वारेज काब्राल ने की थी। अतः केवल A, B और C सही होने से विकल्प A सही उत्तर है।
Q.1Consider the following statements in the context of the background and geographical discoveries of the discovery of a new sea route from Europe to India in the second half of the 15th century:
A.The traditional land route between Europe and Asia was blocked by the capture of Constantinople by the Ottoman Turks (Ottoman Empire) in 1453 A
B.In 1488 AD, Portuguese sailor Bartholomew Dias discovered the southern tip of Africa, which was named 'Cape of Good Hope' (Utamasha Cape). By the 'Treaty of Tordesillas' of
C.1494 AD, the non-Christian world was divided between Spain and Portugal on the instructions of Pope Alexander VI.
D.Spanish sailor Christopher Columbus discovered the coast of Brazil while crossing the Atlantic in his expedition to discover India in 1492 AD.
(a)Only A, B and C✓
(b)Only B, C and D✗
(c)Only A and D✗
(d)A, B, C and D all✗
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
In 1453 AD, due to the capture of Constantinople, the capital of the Byzantine Empire by the Ottoman Turks, Europe's traditional trade route between the Red Sea and the Persian Gulf was closed. After this, as a result of Renaissance and scientific progress in Europe, the search for new sea routes started. With the encouragement of Prince 'Henry the Navigator' of Portugal, Bartholomew Dias reached the southern tip of Africa (Cape of Good Hope/Cape of Storms) in 1488. On June 7, 1494, the 'Treaty of Tordesillas' was signed between Spain and Portugal, under which an imaginary line was drawn 370 leagues west from the Cape Verde Islands and the eastern part was handed over to Portugal and the western part to Spain. Statement D is false because Christopher Columbus, who set out under the flag of Spain in 1492, discovered America (West Indies/Bahamas) while searching for India, while Brazil was discovered by the Portuguese sailor Pedro Alvarez Cabral in 1500 AD. Hence, option A is the correct answer since only A, B and C are correct.

Q.2Which of the following facts is not historically correct regarding Vasco-da-Gama's arrival in India in 1498 AD and his initial travels?
(a)Vasco-da-Gama landed at Kappad port near Calicut (Kozhikode) with his famous ship 'Sao Gabriel' on 20 May 1498.✗
(b)Gujarati Muslim merchant Abdul Manik (Abdul Majeed) played the role of Vasco-da-Gama's guide from the Malindi coast of Africa to Calicut.✗
(c)The local Hindu ruler of Calicut, whose ancestral title was 'Zamorin' (Samuthiri), opposed Vasco-da-Gama and refused to allow him to trade.✓
(d)The pepper and spices that Vasco da Gama took from India to Europe earned him a profit of 60 times the total cost after deducting all the travel expenses.✗
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
On May 20, 1498, Portuguese sailor Vasco-da-Gama reached Kappad coast of Calicut with four ships (the main ship being Sao Gabriel) after completing a sea voyage of 90 days. In this journey, the famous sailor and businessman Abdul Majeed (Abdul Manik) of Gujarat played the role of guide and pilot from Malindi in Africa to India. The then Hindu ruler of Calicut, the Zamorin (Samuthiri), gave a friendly welcome to Vasco-da-Gama and allowed him to keep goods and trade, although the Arab traders already established in Calicut strongly opposed the presence of the Portuguese (hence option C is the correct answer due to incorrect facts). Vasco-da-Gama's sale of spices and pepper taken from India to European markets resulted in a net profit of 60 times the total expense of the entire expedition, which created a direct trade competition with India throughout Europe.

Explanation
With the efforts of Pedro Alvarez Cabral and Alfonso de Albuquerque, the Portuguese established their first trading post in India in Cochin in 1503 AD and the second in Coonoor in 1505 AD. Q.30Consider the following statements with reference to the major reasons for the ultimate success and dominance of the British in India as compared to all other European powers (Portuguese, Dutch, Danish, French):
A.The East India Company was a corporation of private capitalists whose board of directors took independent and prompt commercial decisions, free from political interference.
B.Britain had the world's most powerful and technologically advanced navy (Royal Navy), which maintained control over maritime trade routes and logistics supplies.
C.Due to the Industrial Revolution in Britain, they had immense economic resources and the strong credit system of the Bank of England provided continuous funding for wars.
D.The British first captured the economically most prosperous and fertile province of Bengal (civil and revenue), from which they got unlimited financial and military resources for the conquest of entire India.
(a)Only A, B and C✗
(b)Only B, C and D✗
(c)Only A and D✗
(d)A, B, C and D all✓
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
The success of the British in India was not due to any single cause, but was the result of multidimensional institutional, economic and strategic superiority. Unlike the French and Portuguese companies, the British East India Company was a privately owned autonomous institution without direct political interference from the government. Britain's Royal Navy was the most powerful in both the Atlantic and Indian Ocean, cutting off French supplies. The British were able to fight long-term wars without emptying the treasury because of Britain's stable constitutional government and a national debt market operated by the Bank of England (1694). Most importantly, Dupleix and the French devoted their energies to the relatively less fertile and war-torn areas of the Deccan, while the British captured 'Bengal', India's most wealthy and fertile province after Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764), whose abundant revenues provided them with the wealth and massive army needed to conquer the rest of India. Hence, all four statements A, B, C and D are completely true.
