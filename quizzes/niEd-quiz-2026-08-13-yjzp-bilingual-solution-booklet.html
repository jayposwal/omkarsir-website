Q.1भारतीय संविधान के भाग-6 के अंतर्गत राज्य की कार्यपालिका का संवैधानिक प्रमुख कौन होता है?
(a)मुख्यमंत्री✗
(b)राज्यपाल✓
(c)उच्च न्यायालय का मुख्य न्यायाधीश✗
(d)राज्य का मुख्य सचिव✗
(e)अनुत्तरित प्रश्न✗
व्याख्या
राज्य स्तर पर संसदीय शासन प्रणाली के अंतर्गत दोहरी कार्यपालिका पाई जाती है, जिसमें राज्य का औपचारिक व संवैधानिक प्रमुख राज्यपाल (Governor) होता है जबकि वास्तविक प्रमुख मुख्यमंत्री होता है। संविधान के भाग-6 में अनुच्छेद 153 से 167 तक राज्य कार्यपालिका व राज्यपाल पद से संबंधित उपबंध दिए गए हैं। अनुच्छेद 154(1) के अनुसार राज्य की कार्यपालिका शक्ति राज्यपाल में निहित होती है, जिसका प्रयोग वह स्वयं अथवा अपने अधीनस्थ अधिकारियों के माध्यम से करता है। विकल्प A गलत है क्योंकि मुख्यमंत्री राज्य की कार्यपालिका का वास्तविक प्रमुख होता है। विकल्प C गलत है क्योंकि उच्च न्यायालय का मुख्य न्यायाधीश राज्य का सर्वोच्च न्यायिक अधिकारी होता है, न कि कार्यपालिका प्रमुख। विकल्प D गलत है क्योंकि मुख्य सचिव राज्य प्रशासनिक तंत्र का सर्वोच्च प्रशासनिक अधिकारी होता है।
Q.1Who is the constitutional head of the state executive under Part 6 of the Indian Constitution?
(a)Chief Minister✗
(b)Governor✓
(c)Chief Justice of High Court✗
(d)Chief Secretary of State✗
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
At the state level, under the parliamentary system of governance, dual executive is found, in which the formal and constitutional head of the state is the Governor, while the real head is the Chief Minister. In Part-6 of the Constitution, from Articles 153 to 167, provisions related to the state executive and the post of Governor are given. According to Article 154(1), the executive power of the state is vested in the Governor, which he exercises either himself or through his subordinate officers. Option A is incorrect because the Chief Minister is the actual head of the executive of the state. Option C is incorrect because the Chief Justice of the High Court is the highest judicial officer of the state and not the head of the executive. Option D is incorrect because the Chief Secretary is the highest administrative officer of the state administrative machinery.

Q.2Consider the following statements with reference to the constitutional position of the Governor and the 7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956:
A.According to Article 153 of the original Constitution, there was a provision for only one separate Governor for each State.
B.A provision to appoint the same person as Governor of two or more states was added by the 7th Constitutional Amendment, 1956.
C.By the 7th Constitutional Amendment, A, B, C, D categories of states were abolished and the post of 'Governor' was created in place of 'Rajpramukh'.
D.Gurmukh Nihal Singh became the first Governor of Rajasthan on 1 November 1956.
(a)Only A, B and C✗
(b)Only B, C and D✗
(c)All A, B, C and D✓
(d)Only A and D✗
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
All the statements presented are completely true from historical and constitutional point of view. Article 153 of the original Constitution provided for a separate Governor for each state, which was amended by the 7th Constitutional Amendment in 1956 to provide that the same person could be appointed Governor of two or more states. Under this amendment, A, B, C, D categories of states were abolished by repealing Part 7 and by abolishing the post of Rajpramukh in Rajasthan, Sardar Gurmukh Nihal Singh became the first Governor of the state on 1 November 1956. The role of the Governor is dual; Along with being the constitutional head of the state, he acts as a bridge between the Union and the State as the representative of the Centre. Hence option C is the correct answer.

Q.3Match correctly the state and central level chief posts on the basis of their constitutional/administrative role:
List-IList-II
A. Constitutional Head of the State1. President of India
B. De facto Head of the State2. Prime Minister of India
C. Constitutional Head of the Center3. Governor of a State
D. De facto Head of the Center4. Chief Minister of a State
(a)A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2✓
(b)A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1✗
(c)A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2✗
(d)A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4✗
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
The Indian Constitution adopts a parliamentary system of governance at both the central and state levels, where nominal (constitutional) and actual heads are separate. At the state level, the constitutional head is the Governor (A-3) and the de facto head is the Chief Minister (B-4). Similarly, at the central level, the constitutional head is the President of India (C-1) and the de facto head is the Prime Minister (D-2). Option B is incorrect as it depicts the Chief Minister as the constitutional head of the state. Options C and D give the wrong combination as they do not correctly match the difference between the constitutional and substantive powers of the President and the Governor. Hence option A (A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2) is the correct answer.

Q.4Under which Article of the Indian Constitution the Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal?
(a)Article 153✗
(b)Article 154✗
(c)Article 155✓
(d)Article 156✗
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
Under Article 155 of the Constitution, the Governor of a State is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal. This appointment is practically made on the advice of the Union Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister. In India, this system of appointment by the Center instead of direct or indirect election of the Governor is inspired by the Constitution of Canada. Option A is incorrect because Article 153 provides for the post of Governor of the States. Option B is wrong because Article 154 states that the executive power of the state is vested in the Governor. Option D is incorrect because Article 156 deals with the tenure of the Governor and his holding office during the pleasure of the President.

Q.5Consider the reasons why Canada adopted the centrally appointed governor model rather than a governor elected by the Constituent Assembly:
A.If the governor were elected, there would be a direct conflict of powers between the Chief Minister and the Governor.
B.Due to the elected Governor being associated with any political party, he could promote party politics and separatism in the state.
C.In a parliamentary system the Governor is only the nominal head, hence additional financial expenditure on direct election was considered unnecessary.
D.The appointed Governor was necessary to maintain unity and integrity in India's "centre-tilted" federal system.
(a)Only A and B✗
(b)Only B, C and D✗
(c)Only A, C and D✗
(d)All A, B, C and D✓
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
There was a detailed discussion in the Constituent Assembly on the election versus appointment model of the Governor. Ultimately Dr. B.R. On the basis of the arguments of Ambedkar and Jawaharlal Nehru, the system of appointed Governor based on the Canada model was accepted. If the Governor had been elected by the public, he would have considered himself having public support and would have entered into an administrative confrontation with the elected Chief Minister. Additionally, partisan elections would affect the impartiality of the Governor and impose unnecessary financial burden. In Indian federalism, a Governor appointed by the President was considered the best option for national unity and central supervision over the states. Hence, the four statements A, B, C and D presented are completely relevant and true, hence the correct option is D.

Q.50Consider the following statements with reference to the business conducting committees of the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly and the powers of the Governor:
A.The Petitions Committee, Privileges Committee and Committee on Government Assurances have a maximum of 15 members.
B.The number of members in the General Purposes Committee is not pre-determined.
C.The Governor can dissolve the Assembly, call and adjourn the session, but cannot dismiss the RPSC members.
D.The Governor has the right to suspend the members of Rajasthan Public Service Commission, but only the President can dismiss them.
(a)Only A and B✗
(b)Only B, C and D✗
(c)Only A, C and D✗
(d)All A, B, C and D✓
(e)Question not attempted✗
Explanation
All the statements presented are completely true in the context of the procedure of Rajasthan Legislative Assembly and the constitutional rights of the Governor. The petition, privileges and government assurance committees of the Assembly have a maximum of 15 members, whereas the number in the General Purpose Committee is not fixed. Under Article 174, the Governor calls, adjourns and can dissolve the session. Under Article 317, the Governor can only suspend RPSC members during investigation, but the power to remove/dismiss from the post rests only with the President (on the recommendation of the Supreme Court). Hence, the correct answer is option D (all A, B, C and D).
